Web & business platforms

Business software should make a working process easier to understand, not simply move a spreadsheet into a browser. PREJ builds web platforms, portals and administrative workspaces around the people, records and decisions involved. Our work with React, Next.js and TypeScript connects the visible interface to the underlying data and APIs, so the product can support a complete workflow on desktop and mobile screens.

What we can build together

01

Give each role a clear workspace

An administrator, a member of staff and an external account holder usually need different information and actions. We clarify those roles, permissions and handovers before building the interface. A useful dashboard helps someone decide what to do next; it is not just a wall of numbers. Navigation, forms and record views should reflect the task and explain what has changed after an action.

02

Turn repeated operations into deliberate flows

Scheduling, attendance, document handling, order tracking or communication can involve several linked steps. We identify the source of each record, the fields that need validation and the exceptions that require human review. This is particularly important when a change affects another team member or an exported document. The aim is a maintainable working process with visible boundaries, not an unexplained collection of automations.

03

Build for review and handover

We choose technology around the product and its maintenance needs, then develop the agreed workflows in increments. Reviews cover the full path from data entry to the resulting screen or record. Account access, error messages and responsive layouts are part of that work. External services, migration work, deployment and support are scoped for the project rather than assumed to be included in every platform.

A scope you can review

  • A mapped workflow with user roles, records and acceptance criteria.
  • Responsive pages, forms and administrative views for the agreed scope.
  • Connected data flows, priority journey checks and a documented handover.

From requirements to a working system

We begin by understanding how the work happens today and where it becomes difficult. Together we define a first useful scope, identify dependencies and agree how progress will be reviewed. Architecture, product design and engineering are handled as parts of one system. Human review remains central even when AI assists implementation. Known limitations and the next delivery stage are recorded instead of hidden behind a polished interface.

Practical questions

Can we start with one part of our process?

Yes. A bounded workflow can be a better first stage than replacing every tool at once. We examine its dependencies and agree what must connect now, what can remain manual and what belongs in a later phase.

Will a standard template be enough?

Sometimes an existing tool fits the need. We assess the actual workflow before recommending custom development. Where roles, data relationships or review steps require a tailored platform, the scope should explain why those differences matter.
