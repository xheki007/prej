Project case study · Custom web platform

A connected backoffice and online registration for Home of Dance

PREJ developed a custom web application for Home of Dance to support the organisation of its dance-school operations. The project brings together an internal backoffice and a public course-selection and registration experience for the school’s locations in Breisach and Freiburg. Course discovery, participant information and administrative review are parts of one connected workflow, while the office retains responsibility for confirmation.

What the platform connects

  • Public course discovery and registration requests
  • Course planning, attendance and office worklists
  • Contracts, electronic signing and invoice administration

From finding a course to a reviewed request

The public journey begins with choosing a suitable course, not with a generic contact form. Visitors can narrow the catalogue by location, dance style, age and course type. Availability and waiting-list states help explain which next step is available, and the selection can include more than one course. These controls connect the public interface to the school’s course offering without treating every request as the same transaction.

Structured information, with a clear confirmation boundary

The registration experience distinguishes binding-registration requests, trial requests, enquiries and waiting-list requests. It collects the information needed for the relevant workflow, including participant and guardian contact details, contract or change information, start month, tariff and address. Payment preferences include SEPA direct debit and bank transfer. Privacy information is separate from optional consent to publish photos or videos, rather than bundling those different choices into one unchecked assumption.

A request is not an immediate confirmation

Submitted requests are reviewed by the Home of Dance office. A contract takes effect only when the office confirms it. Selecting a course, a tariff or a payment method does not itself mean that a place has been confirmed or a payment has been collected. That distinction is part of the public workflow and remains important when describing what the software does.

The working side of the system

Behind the public pages, the backoffice groups the tools around the school’s operational tasks. The case study describes the functions present in the application, not a measured claim about time saved or commercial performance.

01

Courses and attendance

Course records include time, location, room and status. A weekly plan supports the timetable view, while attendance is organised by course and date. This gives the administrative interface a concrete connection to the scheduled activity rather than leaving the timetable and participation records as unrelated screens.

02

Registration and booking review

The office worklist supports status, request type, area, responsibility, owner decision and search. Staff can review incoming registrations and bookings in an administrative context. The presence of a public form does not remove that review step or imply automatic acceptance of every submitted request.

03

Contracts and invoices

Contract workflows include PDF preparation, upload, review and billing controls. Electronic contract signing is also part of the platform’s contract functionality. Invoice administration includes month and status views, with settled and open states. These are software features; this case study does not make a legal-validity assurance about signatures or claim that payment transactions were tested.

One platform, distinct responsibilities

The project connects public-facing choices with the information an office needs to work through them. Visitors need a clear route to the right course and a precise explanation of what their request means. The backoffice needs structured records, review states and the related course, contract and invoice context. Keeping those responsibilities visible is a practical design decision: a polished booking screen should not hide who must confirm the request or what happens next.

What this case study shows

The public screenshots show the course catalogue and request interface without private participant information. The feature description covers the observed public journey and the backoffice inventory, with electronic signing confirmed as a project feature. No live registration, payment or signature was submitted during the case-study review. No performance metrics, testimonials or claims of fully autonomous operation are inferred from the interfaces.
